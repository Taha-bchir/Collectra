\chapter*{Abstract}
\phantomsection
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}
\sectionrule

This report presents the final-year internship project carried out at
\textbf{CyberBridge} under the title \textbf{Collectra} --
a web-based platform for debt collection management and customer reminder campaigns.

Debt collection teams commonly rely on fragmented tools: spreadsheets for data
storage, email threads for communication history, and general-purpose Customer
Relationship Management systems that provide no lifecycle tracking for outstanding
debts.
Collectra addresses these gaps by bringing debt records, campaigns, team actions,
and customer communication into a single, workspace-based Software as a Service
environment.

The platform delivers secure authentication, workspace-level data isolation,
role-based access control for managers and agents, a complete debt lifecycle from
import through payment confirmation, automated invoice generation and delivery,
campaign-level email tracking, and an in-app support chatbot.

Development followed the Scrum agile methodology across four two-week sprints.

\medskip
\noindent{\color{accentblue}\rule{2.8cm}{0.55pt}}\par\vspace{0.3em}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:}
debt collection, Software as a Service, campaign management, Scrum,
agile methodology, invoice generation, email tracking.

% =============================================================================

Debt collection is a critical business process for financial institutions, telecom
operators, and service companies that manage large portfolios of unpaid invoices.
Despite its importance, many collection teams still rely on fragmented tools:
spreadsheets for data storage, email threads for communication history, and separate
Customer Relationship Management systems that were never Conceptioned for debt lifecycle
tracking.
The consequences are predictable: poor traceability, weak team collaboration, and no
single operational view of campaign progress.

The \textbf{Collectra} project was developed during this internship at CyberBridge
specifically to address these gaps.
Collectra is a Software as a Service web platform that brings debt data, campaigns,
team activities, and customer communication into one secure, workspace-based
environment.
It replaces scattered tools with a single, role-aware application that supports the
full debt lifecycle, from initial import to payment confirmation and invoice delivery.

This report is organized as follows:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Chapter 1 -- Company Presentation and Project Context}:
        host company, problem statement, analysis of existing solutions, and
        proposed solution.
  \item \textbf{Chapter 2 -- Requirements and Specifications}:
        actors, functional and non-functional requirements, Scrum planning,
        quality assurance, security architecture, general architecture, deployment
        infrastructure, and end-to-end user journeys.
  \item \textbf{Chapter 3 -- Sprint 1: Core Authentication and Workspace Setup}:
        authentication system, workspace management, and member invitation flow.
  \item \textbf{Chapter 4 -- Sprint 2: Campaign and Debt Management}:
        campaign lifecycle, CSV import, debt status tracking, and customer personal
        links.
  \item \textbf{Chapter 5 -- Sprint 3: Collaboration and Reporting}:
        action history, payment promise recording, and the operational manager
        dashboard.
  \item \textbf{Chapter 6 -- Sprint 4: Payment, Invoicing, and Analytics}:
        payment confirmation, multi-currency Stripe checkout, invoice generation,
        email tracking, and final analytics integration.
  \item \textbf{Chapter 7 -- Cross-Cutting Technical Topics}:
        email pipeline, payment flow, in-app support chatbot, error handling,
        and platform-wide engineering practices.
  \item \textbf{Appendices}:
        domain glossary, interface walkthrough with screenshots, API reference,
        and database schema.
\end{itemize}

\section{Project Context}

\subsection{Main Issue}

Debt collection teams face a recurring set of operational challenges.
Follow-up work is often fragmented across multiple tools: debt data is stored in
spreadsheets, contact history is tracked in email threads, and reminder statuses are
maintained manually.
This makes it difficult to audit past actions, share information across team members,
or monitor campaign progress in real time.

The specific problems identified at the start of the project were:
\begin{itemize}
  \item scattered debt data without a single source of truth;
  \item weak traceability of reminders, promises, and payments;
  \item limited collaboration between agents and managers;
  \item no unified view of campaign progress or debt lifecycle status;
  \item no secure way for customers to consult their own debt information.
\end{itemize}

The central question is therefore: \emph{how can we build a secure, scalable platform
that centralizes debt collection work while keeping data strictly isolated per
workspace?}

\subsection{Stakeholder Expectations}

During requirements workshops at CyberBridge, three expectations guided the product
direction.
\textbf{Managers} needed a reliable overview of campaign progress, team activity, and
payment outcomes without exporting spreadsheets.
\textbf{Agents} needed fast daily operations: importing debts, updating statuses,
generating customer links, and recording promises with minimal friction.
\textbf{Customers} needed a simple, trustworthy page to understand their debt and
complete payment without creating an account.
These expectations translated directly into the sprint backlog and into non-functional
requirements (security, traceability, and performance under bulk import).

\subsection{Analysis of Existing Solutions}

A study of available tools revealed the following limitations:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Spreadsheets (Excel, Google Sheets)}: flexible but difficult to
        audit, with no role governance, no lifecycle tracking, and no secure
        customer access;
  \item \textbf{Generic CRM tools (Salesforce, HubSpot)}: Conceptioned for sales
        pipelines, not debt status lifecycles; often too complex or costly for
        small collection teams;
  \item \textbf{Manual link sharing}: no expiration control, no authentication,
        and no audit trail.
\end{itemize}

The proposed solution is \textbf{Collectra}, a workspace-based SaaS platform that
covers all five critical capabilities that existing tools only address partially,

\section{Collectra Platform Requirements}

\subsection{Identification of the Actors}

The Collectra platform involves human actors and three external system services:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Owner}: creates a workspace at registration (without an invitation
        token), holds full control including workspace deletion, and may invite members;
  \item \textbf{Manager}: inherits all \textbf{Agent} capabilities and may
        additionally invite team members (agents cannot send invitations);
  \item \textbf{Agent}: accepts an invitation or registers as owner, authenticates,
        creates campaigns, imports debts, and monitors email tracking; the system
        sends customer emails via Brevo---agents do not manually distribute personal
        links;
  \item \textbf{Customer}: receives a personal link by email, opens it without an
        account, pays via Stripe or sets a promise date; overdue status is applied
        automatically by scheduled jobs when deadlines pass without payment;
  \item \textbf{System services} (external integrations): \textbf{Supabase}
        (authentication, Google OAuth, PostgreSQL hosting), \textbf{Brevo}
        (transactional email and delivery tracking webhooks), and \textbf{Stripe}
        (hosted Checkout and payment webhooks as the payment source of truth).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Functional Requirements}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Secure user authentication and session management;
  \item workspace creation and strict workspace-based data isolation;
  \item team invitation and role-based permission assignment;
  \item campaign creation, configuration, and lifecycle management;
  \item bulk CSV import for debt records;
  \item debt status tracking across a defined lifecycle;
  \item generation of JWT-secured customer personal links (30-day expiration) and
        automated delivery through Brevo after CSV import;
  \item customer debt consultation via personal link (no account required);
  \item Stripe payment confirmation with idempotent verification;
  \item invoice generation and delivery after confirmed payment;
  \item dashboard indicators for campaign and debt monitoring;
  \item action history log for full traceability;
  \item campaign-level email tracking analytics;
  \item per-debt currency selection for CSV import, debt records, and Stripe
        Checkout;
  \item in-app support chatbot for managers and agents (OpenRouter with rule-based
        fallback).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Non-Functional Requirements}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Security}: JWTs signed with HMAC-SHA-256, HTTP-only cookies,
        and route-level middleware checks for every protected endpoint;
  \item \textbf{Maintainability}: modular monorepo architecture with shared
        packages and a single Prisma schema as the source of truth;
  \item \textbf{Performance}: efficient handling of bulk CSV imports with Prisma
        transaction batching to avoid database timeouts;
  \item \textbf{Reliability}: validated API contracts via Zod, database-level
        constraints, and idempotent payment confirmation logic;
  \item \textbf{Usability}: clean dashboard workflow with a minimal learning curve,
        validated through iterative stakeholder feedback during each sprint review.
\end{itemize}

\section{Quality Assurance and Testing Strategy}
\label{sec:qa}

Quality assurance was integrated into every sprint rather than postponed to the end
of the internship.
The approach combined \textbf{functional tests} (documented in each sprint chapter),
\textbf{contract validation} with Zod on the backend, and \textbf{manual exploratory
testing} on Vercel preview deployments before merging to the production branch.

\subsection{Testing Levels}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Unit-level checks}: validation helpers, status-transition guards, and
        payment verification utilities were exercised with representative inputs and
        edge cases;
  \item \textbf{Integration tests}: API endpoints were verified with Postman
        collections and Swagger UI, focusing on authentication cookies, workspace
        isolation, and import summaries;
  \item \textbf{End-to-end flows}: login, workspace invitation, CSV import, customer
        personal link access, Stripe checkout, and invoice retrieval were replayed on
        preview environments after each sprint review;
  \item \textbf{Regression discipline}: critical paths from earlier sprints were
        re-tested when shared middleware or database schema changed.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Test Data and Acceptance Criteria}

Each sprint defined explicit acceptance criteria in Linear (expected status codes,
visible UI states, and database side effects).
For imports, tests included files with delimiter variations, duplicate emails, invalid
amounts, and large batches.
For payments, tests covered duplicate verification calls to confirm idempotent
confirmation.

\subsection{Defect Management}

Tasks assigned by the supervisor were managed in Linear with priority levels and
details about the affected module (\texttt{apps/web}, \texttt{apps/api}, or shared
packages).
High-priority issues were completed first; less important improvements were deferred
to the backlog with clear priority labels.

\section{Security Architecture}
\label{sec:security}

Security was treated as a cross-cutting concern from Sprint~1 onward.
Collectra combines authentication, authorization, and tenant isolation at multiple
layers.

\subsection{Authentication and Session Model}

Users authenticate through Supabase Auth.
The backend issues a signed JWT stored in an \textbf{HTTP-only} session cookie so
client-side scripts cannot read it.
Protected routes verify the token on every request before executing business logic.

\subsection{Workspace Isolation and Role-Based Access Control}

All operational data is scoped to a \texttt{workspaceId}.
Middleware rejects cross-tenant access attempts with \texttt{403 Forbidden} even when
a user is authenticated.
\textbf{Managers} (and owners) may invite members; \textbf{agents} cannot.
Campaign supervision, email-tracking analytics, and sensitive workspace actions are
restricted to manager or owner roles according to API middleware rules.

\subsection{Customer-Facing Security}

Personal links rely on signed JWTs with expiration.
Public endpoints never expose internal identifiers without verification.
Payment confirmation uses Stripe session identifiers, database-level locks, and
idempotent verification to prevent duplicate charges.

\subsection{Secrets and Deployment Hygiene}

Database credentials, JWT secrets, Stripe keys, and Brevo credentials are injected as
environment variables in Vercel.
No secret is committed to Git.
Preview deployments use separate configuration so test keys cannot affect production
data.

\section{General Architecture of the Application}

Collectra is structured as a \textbf{monorepo} managed with pnpm and Turborepo.
It contains four main packages:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \texttt{apps/web} -- the Next.js frontend application;
  \item \texttt{apps/api} -- the Hono backend REST API;
  \item \texttt{packages/database} -- the shared Prisma client and database schema;
  \item \texttt{packages/types} -- shared TypeScript types and Zod validation schemas.
\end{itemize}

The logical architecture separates four main domains: authentication and session
management, workspace and team management, campaign and debt management, and customer
access.
Each domain maps to a dedicated set of API routes and frontend pages.

\subsection{Monorepo Structure and Code Organisation}

The monorepo is orchestrated with Turborepo task pipelines so unchanged packages are
skipped during continuous integration.
Shared types in \texttt{packages/types} prevent drift between the Next.js client and
the Hono server: when a request schema changes, TypeScript compilation fails until
both sides are updated.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Frontend (\texttt{apps/web})}: route groups for authentication,
        workspace dashboards, campaign screens, and customer-facing public pages;
  \item \textbf{Backend (\texttt{apps/api})}: route modules grouped by domain (auth,
        workspaces, campaigns, debts, public customer access, payments);
  \item \textbf{Database (\texttt{packages/database})}: Prisma schema, migrations,
        and generated client;
  \item \textbf{Shared contracts (\texttt{packages/types})}: Zod schemas reused for
        validation and OpenAPI generation.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Data Persistence and Domain Entities}

PostgreSQL stores all business entities with strict foreign keys between workspaces,
campaigns, debts, promises, and history tables.
Prisma enforces referential integrity and provides typed queries for dashboard
aggregations and bulk imports.
Customer-facing actions are persisted in history tables so managers can audit who
changed a status, when a promise was recorded, and when a payment was confirmed.

\section{Deployment and CI/CD Pipeline}
\label{sec:deployment}

\subsection{Hosting Infrastructure}

Collectra is deployed on \textbf{Vercel} under the custom domain
\texttt{collectra.xyz}.
The Supabase-managed PostgreSQL database runs on Supabase's cloud infrastructure,
and all environment variables (database connection strings, JWT secrets, Stripe keys,
Brevo API key) are configured as Vercel project environment variables and are never
committed to the repository.

\subsection{Git Branching Strategy}

The project uses a two-branch Git workflow on GitHub:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{\texttt{develop}}: the active development branch where all feature
        branches are merged after code review;
  \item \textbf{\texttt{production}}: the stable branch that drives live deployments;
        a pull request from \texttt{develop} into \texttt{production} is opened at
        the end of each sprint after all sprint tests pass.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Automated Deployment Pipeline}

Vercel is connected directly to the GitHub repository.
Every merge into \texttt{production} triggers an automatic Vercel build and
deployment:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Developer pushes a feature branch and opens a pull request into
        \texttt{develop}.
  \item After review, the feature branch is merged into \texttt{develop}.
  \item At sprint end, \texttt{develop} is merged into \texttt{production}.
  \item Vercel detects the push, runs the Turborepo build pipeline, and publishes
        the updated application to \texttt{collectra.xyz} automatically.
  \item Vercel also generates a unique preview URL for every pull request, enabling
        stakeholder review before merging.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Manager Journey}

A manager performs the same operational flow as an agent and, in addition, invites

the dashboard and \textbf{email tracking} views.
This journey validates role-based access control, workspace isolation, and reporting

\subsection{Agent Journey}

The typical agent flow is: \textbf{accept invitation} (or register as workspace
owner), \textbf{authenticate}, \textbf{create a campaign}, and \textbf{import a CSV
file} of debts.
After import, the system automatically sends each debtor a Brevo email containing a
signed personal link.
The agent monitors customer reactions through email tracking and action history on the
campaign debt table.

\subsection{Customer Journey}

A customer \textbf{receives the personal link by email}, opens
\texttt{/client/view?token=...} \textbf{without logging in}, reviews the debt amount
and due date, then either clicks \textbf{Pay now securely} (Stripe Checkout) or
\textbf{sets a promise-to-pay date}.
After Stripe payment, the customer is redirected to a verification view and may open
the hosted invoice once the webhook confirms \texttt{PAID}.

\subsection{Sprint 1 Business Logic}

\subsubsection{Authentication}

Authentication is handled by Supabase Auth using email and password.
On a successful login, the backend sets an HTTP-only session cookie containing the
user's JWT.
A Next.js middleware layer checks this cookie on every protected route and page,
redirecting unauthenticated requests to the login screen.
This approach prevents token theft via JavaScript and protects all workspace data
behind a single, centrally managed guard.

\subsubsection{Workspace Isolation}

Every API endpoint that accesses workspace resources validates a \texttt{workspaceId}
guard before processing any data.
The system verifies that the authenticated user is an active member of the requested
workspace, ensuring that data from one workspace is never visible to users of another.

\subsubsection{Invitation Flow}

When a manager invites a user, the system:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item generates a unique, time-limited invitation token;
  \item stores the invitation record with status \textit{Pending} in the database;
  \item sends an email containing a signed acceptance link;
  \item on acceptance, creates the workspace membership with the assigned role
        and marks the invitation as \textit{Accepted}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Sprint 2 Business Logic}

\subsubsection{Debt Lifecycle}

Collectra models each debt as a finite state machine.
Statuses are stored in the database as enumerated values and validated on every update.
The debt lifecycle follows a strict sequence of status transitions:

\begin{center}
  \texttt{Unpaid} $\;\to\;$ \texttt{Notified} $\;\to\;$ \texttt{Promised}
  $\;\to\;$ \texttt{Paid}
\end{center}

A debt may also move to \texttt{OVERDUE\_AFTER\_PROMISE} if \texttt{dueDate} or a
recorded \texttt{promiseDate} passes without payment being confirmed.
Table~\ref{tab:debt-status} lists the main states, their business meaning, and typical
transitions.

\begin{table}[!htbp]
  \centering
  \reporttablesetup
  \begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{|p{2.8cm}|Y|Z|}
    \hline
    \rowcolor{accentblue!10}
    \textbf{Status} & \textbf{Meaning} & \textbf{Typical Next States} \\
    \hline
    Unpaid   & Debt created or imported; collection not yet started &
    Notified, Imported \\
    \hline
    Imported & Debt created from CSV or manual entry; not yet communicated &
    Notified, Overdue \\
    \hline
    Notified & Customer received a link or email; collection is active &
    Promised, Paid, Overdue \\
    \hline
    Promised & Customer recorded a promise-to-pay date on the public page &
    Paid, Overdue, Notified \\
    \hline
    Paid     & Payment confirmed (Stripe or controlled demo flow) &
    Terminal state \\
    \hline
    Overdue  & Promise or due date passed without payment; set by scheduled job &
    Promised, Paid, Notified \\
    \hline
  \end{tabularx}
  \caption{Debt Status Lifecycle -- States and Transitions}
  \label{tab:debt-status}
\end{table}

The backend enforces allowed transitions and prevents invalid jumps; automatic overdue
transitions and promise reminders are triggered by secured cron routes.

\subsubsection{CSV Import Logic}

The CSV import endpoint implements a tolerant, production-ready importer:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item receives the file as a multipart/form-data upload;
  \item detects the delimiter (comma, semicolon, or tab) and parses quoted values
        robustly;
  \item resolves header aliases and validates required columns (full name, email,
        phone, amount);
  \item normalizes fields (email lowercasing, amount parsing) and maps textual status
        values via a canonical mapping;
  \item matches existing clients by case-insensitive email; for unseen emails the
        importer batch-inserts them using \texttt{createMany} in chunks of 500;
  \item inserts debts using \texttt{createMany} in chunked batches inside a
        transaction to avoid timeouts;
  \item returns a detailed import summary (total rows, imported rows, skipped rows
        with reasons) and a list of per-debt Brevo notifications to attempt.
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{JWT Customer Links}

Personal links are signed JWTs.
The token encodes the debt ID in the \texttt{sub} claim and is signed using the server
secret.
Tokens are issued with a default expiry of 30 days (see \texttt{EXPIRY\_DAYS} in
\texttt{apps/api/src/lib/customer-jwt.ts}).
Token verification is stateless and performed by \texttt{verifyCustomerToken} when the
public link is accessed.
Customer actions via the public link are recorded in \texttt{customerActionHistory} and
Brevo event logs.

\subsubsection{Action History}

Every significant operation -- status update, promise recorded, link generated,

Dashboard queries are computed server-side using Prisma \texttt{groupBy} queries.
Debt records are grouped by campaign and status, returning counts for each
combination.
The frontend renders these counts as summary indicator cards and a per-campaign status
breakdown.
Using \texttt{groupBy} at the database level avoids fetching individual records and
eliminates N+1 query patterns that would degrade performance on large campaigns.

\subsubsection{Role-Based Permission Enforcement}

Sensitive actions -- deleting a campaign, modifying workspace settings, changing
member roles -- are protected by a role guard middleware.
The guard reads the requesting user's workspace role from the database and rejects
the request with \texttt{403 Forbidden} if the role is insufficient.
Agents who attempt Manager-only operations receive a clear error response without any
data leak.

\subsection{Sprint 4 Business Logic}

\subsubsection{Payment Confirmation and Idempotency}

The payment pipeline was hardened to prevent duplicate charges and stale frontend
states.
After Stripe redirection, the client calls a verification endpoint and polls until the
database confirms the debt as \texttt{PAID}.
Server-side confirmation is idempotent: repeated webhook or verification calls do not
duplicate state transitions.

The backend enforces the following safeguards:
\begin{itemize}
  \item a \texttt{pendingStripeSessionId} lock to block concurrent checkout sessions;
  \item eligibility checks before Stripe checkout (debt not \texttt{PAID}, valid
        amount, no conflicting open session);
  \item idempotent confirmation that updates debt status only once.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Invoice Generation and Delivery}

Invoice generation occurs only after confirmed payment.
When the debt transitions to \texttt{PAID}, the service creates or reuses a stable
invoice number, exposes a secure public invoice endpoint, and redirects users to the
hosted invoice or printable PDF representation.

Invoice email is sent as a post-confirmation side effect through Brevo, ensuring
payment state remains the primary source of truth even if email delivery fails.

\subsubsection{Multi-Currency Debts and Stripe Checkout}

Each debt record stores an ISO~4217 currency code (default \texttt{usd}).
Agents and managers can set currency when creating or updating a debt, and bulk CSV
imports accept a currency column so an entire campaign batch can be created in euros,
dollars, or another supported code.

The backend normalizes currency values through \texttt{normalizeStripeCurrency()},
which falls back to the workspace default \texttt{STRIPE\_CURRENCY} environment
variable when a value is missing.
Stripe Checkout uses the debt currency for \texttt{price\_data.currency} and converts
the decimal amount to the smallest currency unit
(\texttt{unit\_amount = round(amount $\times$ 100)} for two-decimal currencies such
as USD and EUR).

\subsubsection{Email Tracking and Analytics Synchronisation}

Campaign import and communication flows now persist normalized tracking actions
(\texttt{EMAIL\_SENT}, \texttt{LINK\_CLICKED}, and mapped provider events) into action
history.
Manager endpoints aggregate these events into operational metrics and refresh them in
near real time.

The final dashboard therefore combines:
\begin{itemize}
  \item debt lifecycle indicators;
  \item payment and invoice completion visibility;
  \item communication performance metrics for campaign follow-up decisions.
\end{itemize}

Key \texttt{<<include>>} relationships express mandatory sub-flows: \textit{Pay Debt} triggers a Stripe checkout session whose confirmation automatically sends a payment receipt via Brevo; \textit{Break Payment Promise} notifies the customer through Brevo; and \textit{View Dashboard} aggregates action history and communication tracking data to present real-time collection metrics.

% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{In-App Support Chatbot}

Collectra includes a floating \textbf{support chatbot} available to authenticated
managers and agents from the dashboard layout.
The widget opens a panel where users type questions about campaigns, CSV import,
overview statistics, account settings, payment links, and invoices.

The web application sends each question to \texttt{POST /api/support/chat}, a
Next.js route that calls the \textbf{OpenRouter} API with a fixed system prompt
describing Collectra capabilities.
The default model is \texttt{deepseek/deepseek-chat-v3-0324}; configuration uses
\texttt{OPENROUTER\_API\_KEY}, \texttt{OPENROUTER\_API\_BASE\_URL}, and related
environment variables.

When the remote model is unavailable or returns an error, the client falls back to a
\textbf{rule-based assistant}: keyword patterns suggest relevant answers and deep
links (for example \texttt{/campaigns}, \texttt{/overview}, \texttt{/settings/account}).
Quick-reply chips and a reset action keep the experience usable without reloading the
page.

The chatbot is intentionally scoped as \textbf{operational guidance}, not autonomous
debt processing: it does not modify database records or trigger payments.

% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Email and Notification Pipeline}

Collectra uses Brevo for transactional email and event tracking.
When agents trigger customer communications, the platform records outbound messages
and synchronises provider events (\texttt{EMAIL\_SENT}, \texttt{LINK\_CLICKED}) into
action history for manager review.
A failed provider callback does not corrupt payment status, but the event log helps
support teams investigate delivery issues.

% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Payment, Invoice, and Customer Return Flow}

The customer journey on a personal link follows a controlled sequence: view debt
details, optionally submit a promise-to-pay date, start Stripe Checkout, return to a
verification endpoint, and access a hosted invoice once payment is confirmed.
Server-side locks prevent parallel checkout sessions for the same debt.
Verification is idempotent: repeated calls after success return the same final state
without duplicate charges or history entries.

% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Error Handling and User Feedback}

API errors return structured JSON payloads with stable error codes.
The frontend maps these codes to actionable messages (invalid import row, expired
link, forbidden workspace access).
For long-running imports, the backend returns per-row skip reasons so agents can
correct files instead of failing the entire batch silently.

% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Performance Considerations}

Large CSV files are processed in chunks inside database transactions.
Dashboard indicators rely on aggregation queries rather than per-debt loops.
Preview deployments on Vercel allowed measuring import duration and dashboard load
time before promoting changes to production.

% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Documentation and Knowledge Transfer}

OpenAPI documentation is generated from Zod schemas, keeping the contract close to
runtime validation.
Sprint reviews at CyberBridge served as living documentation: each demo explained
assumptions, limitations, and follow-up tasks recorded in Linear for continuity after
the internship.

% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Observability and Operational Monitoring}

Although full observability stacks were out of scope, the team adopted lightweight
practices suitable for a Vercel-hosted monorepo.
Build logs and deployment previews on Vercel provide the first line of diagnosis when
a merge fails.
API routes log structured errors server-side (validation code, workspace identifier,
route name) without leaking secrets to clients.
For payment flows, verification endpoints return explicit states (\texttt{pending},
\texttt{confirmed}, \texttt{failed}) so the frontend can stop polling
deterministically.

% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Data Protection and Privacy Considerations}

Collectra stores personal data (customer emails, debt amounts, communication history).
Workspace isolation ensures agents never access another tenant's records.
Customer links are time-limited and signed; expired tokens return
\texttt{TOKEN\_EXPIRED} without revealing whether the identifier ever existed.
HTTP-only cookies protect session tokens from cross-site scripting in the browser.

\textbf{Non-functional requirements validation.}
All five non-functional requirements were met:
\begin{itemize}

        cookies, and verified by middleware on every protected route.
        Workspace isolation is enforced at every layer.
  \item \textbf{Maintainability:} the monorepo structure with a single shared Prisma
        schema and shared Zod types means that any model change propagates
        consistently across frontend and backend without manual synchronization.
  \item \textbf{Performance:} the CSV import uses Prisma transaction batching to
        avoid per-row round trips, and the dashboard uses \texttt{groupBy}
        aggregation to prevent N+1 queries on large campaign datasets.
  \item \textbf{Reliability:} Zod validates every API input at runtime, and the
        idempotent payment confirmation logic prevents duplicate state transitions.
  \item \textbf{Usability:} iterative sprint reviews with CyberBridge stakeholders
        confirmed that the workflow from campaign creation to payment confirmation
        requires minimal training for new collection agents.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Challenges encountered.}
The main challenges faced during the project were:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Conceptioning a flexible but strict debt status transition system that prevents
        invalid lifecycle jumps while remaining easy to extend;
  \item handling large CSV files efficiently without overloading the database or
        causing request timeouts;
  \item securing the Stripe payment return path to avoid duplicate payments while
        keeping customer feedback responsive during webhook delays;
  \item ensuring correct workspace isolation at every layer of the application.
\end{itemize}
Each challenge was resolved through careful schema Conception, server-side validation,
and iterative testing with real data.

\textbf{Personal learning outcomes.}
This internship provided hands-on experience in full-stack TypeScript development,
agile project management with Scrum, and professional software delivery in a real
company context.
It deepened my understanding of authentication flows, role-based access control,
JWT-based security, relational data modeling, and production deployment with Vercel
and GitHub CI/CD pipelines.

\enlargethispage{2\baselineskip}
\textbf{Perspectives and future work.}
Several improvements could extend the Collectra platform in the future:
\begin{itemize}
  \item integration with SMS or WhatsApp for direct, automated customer notifications;
  \item campaign report export in PDF or Excel format;
  \item a mobile-responsive reConception or dedicated mobile application;
  \item advanced analytics and collection performance reporting for managers.
\end{itemize}

In conclusion, Collectra meets all the objectives set at the beginning of the
internship.
It provides collection teams with a secure, structured, and easy-to-use platform
that replaces fragmented tools with a single, purpose-built solution for debt
management.

\chapter{Domain Glossary}
\label{app:glossary}

This glossary defines recurring business and technical terms used in the report.

\begin{description}[style=nextline, leftmargin=3.2cm, labelwidth=2.8cm, itemsep=7pt]
  \item[Workspace]       Isolated tenant environment containing campaigns, debts,
                         members, and history.
  \item[Campaign]        Collection operation grouping debts that share communication
                         and reporting context.
  \item[Debt]            Individual amount owed by a customer, with lifecycle status
                         and payment metadata.
  \item[Personal link]   Time-limited, signed URL allowing a customer to view or act
                         on a debt without an account.
  \item[Promise to pay]  Customer commitment date recorded by an agent or submitted
                         through the public link.
  \item[Action history]  Chronological log of operational events (status updates,
                         promises, payments, tracking).
  \item[Idempotent confirmation] Payment verification that yields the same final state
                         when executed multiple times.
  \item[Import summary]  Structured result of a CSV upload (imported, skipped, error
                         reasons).
  \item[Manager dashboard] Aggregated indicators (counts by status, campaign filters)
                         for supervision.
  \item[Preview deployment] Temporary Vercel build used to validate a pull request
                         before production merge.
  \item[REST]            Architectural style used by the Hono API; resources are
                         addressed by nouns (\texttt{/debts}, \texttt{/campaigns})
                         and manipulated with standard HTTP verbs.
  \item[Zod schema]      TypeScript-first validation definition shared between backend
                         handlers and OpenAPI generation.
  \item[Stripe Checkout] Hosted payment page redirecting customers to a secure card
                         entry flow without storing card data in Collectra.
  \item[Webhook]         Server-to-server callback (Stripe, Brevo) notifying Collectra
                         of external events.
  \item[Tenant]          Synonym for workspace in multi-tenant SaaS terminology.
  \item[Dunning]         Systematic process of communicating with customers to recover
                         overdue debts.
  \item[HTTP-only cookie] Session cookie not accessible to JavaScript, mitigating
                         XSS token theft.
\end{description}

\chapter{Domain Glossary}
\label{app:glossary}

This glossary defines recurring business and technical terms used in the report.

\begin{description}[style=nextline, leftmargin=3.2cm, labelwidth=2.8cm, itemsep=8pt]
  \item[Workspace] Isolated tenant environment containing campaigns, debts, members, and history.
  \item[Campaign] Collection operation grouping debts that share communication and reporting context.
  \item[Debt] Individual amount owed by a customer, with lifecycle status and payment metadata.
  \item[Personal link] Time-limited, signed Uniform Resource Locator allowing a customer to view or act on a debt without an account.
  \item[Promise to pay] Customer commitment date recorded by an agent or submitted through the public link.
  \item[Action history] Chronological log of operational events (status updates, promises, payments, tracking).
  \item[Idempotent confirmation] Payment verification that yields the same final state when executed multiple times.
  \item[Import summary] Structured result of a Comma-Separated Values upload (imported, skipped, error reasons).
  \item[Manager dashboard] Aggregated indicators (counts by status, campaign filters) for supervision.
  \item[Preview deployment] Temporary Vercel build used to validate a pull request before production merge.
  \item[Representational State Transfer (REST)] Architectural style used by the Hono Application Programming Interface; resources are addressed by nouns (\texttt{/debts}, \texttt{/campaigns}) and manipulated with standard HTTP verbs.
  \item[Zod schema] TypeScript-first validation definition shared between backend handlers and OpenAPI generation.
  \item[Stripe Checkout] Hosted payment page redirecting customers to a secure card entry flow without storing card data in Collectra.
  \item[Webhook] Server-to-server callback (Stripe, Brevo) notifying Collectra of external events.
  \item[Tenant] Synonym for workspace in multi-tenant Software as a Service terminology.
  \item[Dunning] Systematic process of communicating with customers to recover overdue debts.
  \item[Hypertext Transfer Protocol-only (HTTP-only) cookie] Session cookie not accessible to JavaScript, mitigating cross-site scripting token theft.
\end{description}

  \item \texttt{WorkspaceMember} -- junction table linking users to workspaces with an assigned role;
  \item \texttt{Invitation} -- pending or accepted membership invitations;
  \item \texttt{Campaign} -- debt collection campaigns scoped to a workspace;
  \item \texttt{Debt} -- individual debt records linked to a campaign and debtor;
  \item \texttt{PaymentPromise} -- recorded payment commitment dates for debts;
  \item \texttt{CustomerActionHistory} -- timeline of operational actions (status updates, promises, payments, tracking events);
  \item \texttt{BrevoEventLog} -- provider-level email event logs used for diagnostics and synchronization.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Indexing and Query Patterns}

Dashboard queries aggregate counts grouped by \texttt{status} and \texttt{campaignId}.
Imports batch-insert debts inside transactions to keep partial failures recoverable.
Personal links index \texttt{token} hashes for constant-time lookup on public routes.
These choices were validated on preview deployments with datasets exceeding five thousand
debts per workspace.
